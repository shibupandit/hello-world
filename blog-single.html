What to do in the first 72 hours after a truck accident

Trucking companies send response teams to a crash scene within hours. Here is what to secure first, and the federal deadlines that work against you.

EH
By Eleanor Hartwell Founding Partner · Board Certified, Civil Trial Law
Published
Oct 14, 2026
Legally reviewed by
Marcus Crane
Reading time
8 min

A crash with an 80,000-pound truck is not like a crash with another car. The trucking company and its insurer usually have investigators at the scene before the wreckage is cleared. What you do in the first three days decides which evidence still exists when your case is built.

In this article

    1. Get medical care and document it

    Go to an emergency room or urgent care the same day, even if you feel fine. Adrenaline hides injuries, and symptoms of concussion or spinal injury often appear 24 to 72 hours later.

    Insurers look for gaps. If you wait a week to see a doctor, they will argue the crash did not cause your injuries. Keep every discharge sheet, prescription and bill in one folder.

    2. Secure the evidence before it disappears

    Commercial trucks carry far more data than a passenger car. Most of it belongs to the carrier, and much of it is kept only for a short time.

    • Electronic logging device (ELD) data shows how long the driver had been on duty.
    • Engine control module ("black box") data records speed, braking and throttle in the seconds before impact.
    • Dash-cam and driver-facing camera footage is often overwritten on a loop.
    • Dispatch messages and delivery schedules can show pressure to drive past legal limits.
    • Maintenance and inspection records for brakes, tires and lights.

    A lawyer can send a preservation letter (also called a spoliation letter) that puts the carrier on notice. Once it is received, destroying that evidence can lead to sanctions in court.

    Key point

    Carriers only have to keep hours-of-service records for six months. Without a preservation letter, the most important evidence in your case can be discarded legally before you file.

    3. Understand the hours-of-service rules

    Federal rules limit how long a truck driver can work. A property-carrying driver may drive up to 11 hours after 10 consecutive hours off duty, and may not drive beyond the 14th hour after coming on duty. Log violations are some of the strongest evidence of negligence in a trucking case.

    The driver's logbook often tells the story of the crash before any witness does.

    Eleanor Hartwell

    4. Know the deadlines that apply

    Some deadlines run in hours. Others run in months or years. Missing any of them can weaken or end a claim.

    Deadlines in a Texas commercial truck crash
    WhatDeadlineSource
    Post-crash alcohol test of the driver (qualifying crashes)8 hours49 C.F.R. § 382.303
    Post-crash controlled-substance test32 hours49 C.F.R. § 382.303
    Carrier keeps records of duty status6 months49 C.F.R. § 395.8(k)
    Notice of claim to a Texas government entity6 months, sometimes lessTex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 101.101
    Filing suit for personal injury or wrongful death2 yearsTex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.003

    5. Be careful with the insurance company

    The carrier's adjuster may call within a day or two, sound sympathetic and ask for a recorded statement. You do not have to give one. Anything you say can be used to argue you were partly at fault.

    Texas uses a modified comparative fault rule. If you are found more than 50 percent responsible, you recover nothing. If you are found 20 percent responsible, your recovery is reduced by 20 percent. That is why insurers work hard to shift even a small share of blame.

    1. Take the adjuster's name and number, and say your lawyer will be in touch.
    2. Do not sign medical authorizations or releases.
    3. Do not post about the crash or your injuries on social media.
    4. Report the crash to your own insurer, sticking to the basic facts.

    6. What to gather now

    • The Texas Peace Officer's Crash Report (form CR-3), available from TxDOT
    • Photos of the vehicles, the scene, road conditions and your injuries
    • The truck's USDOT number and the company name on the cab door
    • Names and phone numbers of witnesses
    • A short written account of what happened, made while it is fresh
