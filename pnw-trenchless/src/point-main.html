Quick answer

What is a point repair?

A point repair fixes one damaged section of a sewer line from the inside. The camera finds the exact spot, then a short liner is carried to it on an inflatable packer, pressed against the old pipe and left to cure. The result is a sealed, jointless patch over the damage, while the rest of the line, which is still sound, stays as it is.

Is a point repair right for your pipe?

When one fix is enough, and when it isn't.

A point repair only makes sense if the rest of the line is in good shape. The camera shows us the whole pipe, and we'll show you the footage either way.

How a point repair works

The spot repair, step by step.

From the first camera run to the final one, here's what happens inside your pipe.

  1. Camera inspection

    We record the full line, check that the rest of the pipe is sound, and measure the exact distance to the damage.

  2. Clean the spot

    Roots, scale and debris come out with hydro jetting / cutting so the patch bonds to a clean wall.

  3. Wet out the patch

    A liner is cut to the length of the damage and saturated with resin type on site.

  4. Position the packer

    The patch rides in on an inflatable packer through the cleanout or a small access point, guided by the camera footage.

  5. Inflate & cure

    The packer presses the patch against the old pipe while the resin hardens, X hours. Then it's deflated and pulled out.

  6. Verify

    A final camera run proves the spot is sealed. You get the before and after footage.

Is a point repair a permanent fix?

For the spot it covers, yes: the patch seals the damage with a jointless liner. It doesn't fix the rest of the line, which is why we check the whole pipe on camera first. Expected life and PNW warranty terms.

Why not just line the whole pipe?

If the rest of the line is sound, you don't need to pay for it. If the camera shows damage in several places, we'll tell you, and lining the whole run may be the better deal.

Do you have to dig?

Usually not. The patch goes in through a cleanout. When a small access point is needed.

How long does it take?

Typical time on site, and how long the line is out of service.

Will roots come back at that joint?

The patch covers the joint the roots got in through, so there's no gap left at that spot. Mike's experience with roots at other joints.

Can you fix more than one spot?

Yes, each damaged spot can get its own patch. Past X spots, lining the whole line often costs about the same, and we'll show you both options.
