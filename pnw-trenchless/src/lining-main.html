Quick answer

What is CIPP pipe lining?

Cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) lining is a trenchless sewer repair. A resin-saturated liner is pulled or inverted into the cleaned host pipe, inflated against the walls and cured until it hardens into a jointless new pipe. Most residential jobs need little or no digging.

Is lining right for your pipe?

When lining works, and when it doesn't.

Lining needs a host pipe that still holds its shape. The camera tells us which side of the line yours is on, and we'll show you the footage either way.

Good candidates

  • Cracks and pinhole leaks
  • Root intrusion at joints
  • Offset or separated joints (minor)
  • Corroded cast iron that's still round
  • Lines under driveways, trees, patios or slabs

Usually a different fix

  • Collapsed or crushed sectionsPipe bursting
  • Orangeburg (fiber pipe) that's deformedPipe bursting
  • Bellies or sags holding waterSpot excavation
  • Pipe too small for the householdBursting to upsize
How CIPP lining works

The lining process, step by step.

From the first camera run to the final one, here's what happens inside your pipe.

  1. Camera inspection

    We record the full line, measure its length and mark every defect and connection.

  2. Clean the pipe

    Roots, scale and debris come out with hydro jetting / descaling so the liner bonds to a clean wall.

  3. Wet out the liner

    The liner is cut to length and saturated with resin type on site.

  4. Install

    We invert / pull it into place through the cleanout or a small access pit and inflate it against the old pipe.

  5. Cure

    The resin hardens (ambient / hot water / steam / UV) into a rigid new pipe in X hours.

  6. Reopen & verify

    Branch connections are reinstated and a final camera run proves the result. You get the footage.

How long does a lined pipe last?

Design life per the liner manufacturer + PNW warranty terms.

Can roots grow through a liner?

A cured liner forms a seamless, jointless pipe, so roots have no joints to get back in through. We clear the roots first, then line the pipe.

Draft · Mike to confirm
Can I use my water and toilets during the job?

How long the line is out of service during lining and curing.

Will lining make my pipe smaller?

Slightly, by the liner's wall thickness. Its smooth, jointless wall usually flows as well as or better than the rough old pipe.

Draft · Mike to confirm
Can you line a pipe that has a belly in it?

A belly (sag) that holds water is usually better fixed by digging that one spot. The camera shows us, and we'll tell you before we start.

Draft · Mike to confirm
Can you line cast iron under my house or slab?

Corroded cast iron that's still round is a good lining candidate, including under slabs. Mike to confirm limits.

Is pipe lining or pipe bursting better?

It depends on the pipe. Lining needs a host pipe that still holds its shape. For collapsed or deformed pipe, or to upsize, pipe bursting pulls in a brand-new HDPE pipe.
