The basics

What is trenchless pipe repair?

Instead of digging a trench the full length of your sewer or water line, we open one or two small access points and repair or replace the pipe underground. Your lawn, driveway, trees and landscaping stay where they are.

There are two main ways to do it. Pipe lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. Pipe bursting pulls a brand-new pipe through while breaking the old one apart. Which one you need depends on what the camera shows.

Sewer Pipe Lining (CIPP)

A resin liner cures inside your old pipe, creating a seamless, jointless pipe within a pipe.

Best for: cracks, leaks, root intrusion, pipes that still hold their shape

Pipe Bursting

A bursting head breaks the old pipe outward while new HDPE pipe is pulled in behind it. A full replacement, not a patch.

Best for: collapsed, Orangeburg or clay lines, or upsizing

Trenchless vs. traditional

Trenchless vs. dig-and-replace: what's the real difference?

The repair price is only part of the cost. With a traditional dig you also pay to restore what came out of the ground.

FactorTrenchless (PNW Trenchless)Traditional excavation
Digging1–2 small access pointsTrench the full length of the pipe
Lawn, driveway, treesLeft intact in most casesRemoved, then rebuilt
Typical timelineX day(s)X days to weeks
Restoration costsMinimalLandscaping, concrete and paving extra
Finished pipeSeamless, root-resistant, X-yr design lifeNew pipe, with joints
Disruption to your homeWater off for hours, not daysYard torn up for the duration

Trenchless isn't right for every pipe. If a section has a belly (sag) or is out of grade, digging that spot may be the better fix, and we'll tell you so.

A cured-in-place liner forms a seamless, jointless pipe inside the old one. With no joints, roots have nowhere to get in. Design life and liner spec.
